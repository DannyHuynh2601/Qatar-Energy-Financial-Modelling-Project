# Qatar-Energy-Financial-Modelling-Project
Qatar Energy aims to deliver a cost-effective, financially advantageous method for supplying natural gas to Bahrain over a 30-year period. Using our financial model, we can compare the trade-off between the high up-front capital cost of the pipeline ($520 million) and the operational flexibility of the Q-Max LNG Tanker ($250 million). The goal is to maximize shareholder wealth by selecting the delivery method (i.e., Pipeline or Tanker) that offers the highest capital efficiency and other return metrics, ensuring that these returns exceed the 4% cost of capital provided by the National Bank of Qatar. 

Executive Summary:
Based on our 30-year competitive financial analysis, the Q-Max Tanker (LNG) is the recommended delivery method. When comparing the 30-year NPV, it outperforms the pipeline across our primary financial metrics, offering higher wealth and faster capital recovery for investors.

Key Insights & Risks							
Operational Resilience: In Year 3, the port damage was 90% covered by insurance, therefore, an additional $500,000 to repair the port had to be paid by Qatar Energy. Despite the port damage, net income increased from Year 2 of ~80 million to Year 3 of ~81 million. Additionally, with carbon costs doubling in Year 5 to over $74 million, the Tanker remains highly profitable, with Net Income in Year 5 of ~47 million. Confirming these situations indicates our model can withstand economic shifts without threatening the project's success. 							
							
							
							
Cash Position: By the end of Year 5, the Tanker generates an ending cash balance of $368,806,012, which exceeds the required $10 million minimum reserve. 							
							
Capital Efficiency (PI & IRR):  The tanker requires an initial capital of 250 million, which is 52% less than the Pipeline's initial investment of $520 millipn. The tanker method provides a PI of 7.92 (compared to the pipeline PI of 3.26), indicating it is twice as efficient an investment and would provide a 7.92x return per dollar. 							
							
							
Wealth (NPV): The tanker (NPV of 1,980,071,025) creates $286,201,680 more in total NPV (of 1,693,869,345) than the pipeline, due to its lower initial investment and the favourable price-to-volume ratio of LNG. 							
							
Cash Recovery (Payback): The tanker reaches a break-even point in 2.52 years of operations, whereas the pipeline takes 5.72 years, representing significantly lower/higher capital-lockup risk. 							
							
Recomendation: 							
It is recommended that Qatar Energy proceed with the Q-Max Tanker delivery method. It offers higher NPV and PI, indicating that Qatar Energy maximizes the value of its sovereign natural gas assets while maintaining superior liquidity and capital flexibility.
